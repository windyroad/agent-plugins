- **P424, 2026-07-26 — fifth witness; front-loading worked, and the remaining cost was gate mechanics, not review substance.** A mechanical character substitution across four surfaces. Front-loaded architect + jtbd per P431, and pre-empted P438's grounding-job blocker and P439's build-upon guard **in the first prompt of each** — both landed: the JTBD gate demanded the new job anyway (JTBD-303) but withdrew the build-upon guard in round one instead of round three. 11 reviewer spawns (3 architect + 3 voice-tone + 2 jtbd + 1 style-guide + 1 accessibility + 1 risk-scorer), five artefacts. Two notes worth more than the count. **(1) The review rounds converged on substance in 3 architect / 2 jtbd rounds; the extra spawns were all gate-marker mechanics** — a genuine fresh-spawn architect PASS whose output led with a `##` heading rather than the bold form never wrote its marker, and three reviewers could not write their own verdict files at all. **(2) The reviews earned their cost.** Architect round 1 found a seventh render site in a third package that the ticket did not name (so the changeset was two packages, not three), four test/eval surfaces the fix would turn RED, an ADR Confirmation criterion pinning the shape being changed, and a deferred fifth surface that is a fail-open emitter/parser pair. Round 2 found that queuing the approach choice without naming where the answer lands is itself an undocumented decision. - **P417, 2026-07-26 — the counterpoint: a doc-only iter clears the wall entirely, and still costs 9 reviewer spawns.** Reconciling two governance indices (story tier + RFC tier) touched no `packages/` path, so ADR-071/089/095/090/096 never engaged and both halves committed inside the iteration. Useful calibration in both directions. **The wall is specific to code, not to governance ceremony** — if a ticket's actionable part can be expressed as a doc/index change, it lands, and it is worth checking whether it can before planning a hold. **But the reviews are not cheaper for being doc-only**: 2 architect + 2 jtbd + 2 pipeline + 2 external-comms + 1 voice-tone. The re-review round was self-inflicted and avoidable — committing the story tier touched four `docs/jtbd/` files, which re-locked the JTBD gate for the RFC half. The architect rounds again earned their cost, catching an ADR-014 per-tier commit-grain violation (one commit for two tiers), an ADR-026 fabrication risk (44 synthesised WSJF values), a curated narrative sentence about to be deleted with a placeholder, an off-by-two row count, and the fact that the new table's PLACEMENT was load-bearing for the detector's section arithmetic. <!-- signal-score: 2 | last-classified: 2026-07-26 | first-written: 2026-07-26 -->
- **P438, 2026-07-26 — fourth witness; front-loading is not enough on its own.** This iter front-loaded architect + jtbd before the first Write as the P431 lesson says, and still paid 4 architect + 3 jtbd rounds, 11 reviewer spawns total. Two new blocking conditions, neither anticipated. **(1) The JTBD gate can refuse the whole change set until a new grounding job exists.** It ruled that neither of the story's acceptance criteria mapped to any documented desired outcome, and that amending the two nearest jobs was worse, because both are `human-oversight: confirmed` and amending them triggers the P357 downgrade-and-re-ratify cycle. Artefact count went to five: map + RFC + story + job + the ticket transition. **(2) Writing that JTBD file immediately invalidates the JTBD gate marker** — `docs/jtbd/` is a policy path, so policy-file drift re-locks the gate and the next gated Write blocks. Sequence jtbd-policy writes LAST among the gated writes, or budget the extra round. Each architect round again surfaced a genuinely new blocker: an acceptance criterion pinning the amending instrument, a defence paragraph copied from a sibling RFC whose premise was false in the new context, and missing reverse-trace tables. <!-- signal-score: 2 | last-classified: 2026-07-26 | first-written: 2026-07-26 -->
